If you are thinking but not writing, you only *think* you're thinking. Writing
is the cheapest way to find out whether an idea survives contact with language.

Here are a few rules that helped me.

## 1. Keep a capture inbox

Most drafts start as a single line in a text file:

- "why static blogs outlive dynamic ones"
- "the case for measuring less"

The bar for adding a line is nearly zero. The bar for turning a line into a
post is "when it keeps coming back".

## 2. Write the ugly version first

A complete, honest, ugly draft beats a beautiful paragraph you never finish.
Editing is a different cognitive task than generating — don't make one block
the other.

```markdown
draft v1: just get the skeleton down
draft v2: make the argument coherent
draft v3: cut everything that isn't load-bearing
```

## 3. One idea per post

If the title needs "and" in it, it's probably two posts. Short posts that
actually ship beat epic series that don't.

## 4. Publish on a schedule you can keep

Weekly is aggressive for most people. Once a fortnight, consistently, for a
year, produces more writing than daily sprints followed by burnout.

> Aim for a pace you could hold for ten years, then subtract half.
